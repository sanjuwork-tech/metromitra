# Strategy and originality

## Start from product truth

Before choosing a visual style, answer:

1. Who must act?
2. What problem is urgent for them?
3. What promise can the product prove?
4. What is the primary conversion?
5. Which objections prevent it?
6. Which real assets or evidence exist?
7. What must the audience remember after ten seconds?

If these answers are missing, make the smallest reversible assumption and label it. Never fabricate business facts.

## Define a creative thesis

A strong thesis connects business strategy with a spatial idea. Examples:

- A financial tool rendered as an evidence room, where every claim is supported by visible data.
- A learning platform staged as a field notebook, with progress emerging through annotations and layered study artifacts.
- A logistics company expressed as a live route system, with content moving through a precise operational map.

Avoid directions such as "modern, clean, premium, minimal." They describe quality, not identity.

## Build a motif from the brand

Find one or two reusable sources:

- logo geometry;
- product behaviour;
- industry material;
- customer workflow;
- physical environment;
- distinctive data;
- language or cultural context.

Translate the motif across composition, media, motion, and micro-detail. Do not paste the logo shape repeatedly.

## Use references without cloning

For each selected reference, write:

| Decision | Extract | Transform | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Composition | Underlying rhythm | Adaptation to product content | Brand-specific signature |
| Type | Scale relationship | Available typeface and voice | Proprietary font imitation |
| Motion | Timing principle | Product-specific interaction | Decorative choreography |
| Surface | Hierarchy logic | Brand palette | Exact colour treatment |

Blend references only when each has a distinct job. Combining five unrelated styles produces visual noise, not originality.

## Use a novelty budget

Make one or two moments genuinely surprising:

- a spatial hero;
- a distinctive transition;
- an interactive proof device;
- an art-directed product gallery;
- an unusual editorial rhythm.

Keep navigation, forms, pricing, and critical conversion controls familiar enough to use without interpretation.

## Test distinctiveness

The design fails the originality test when:

- swapping the logo and accent colour would make it fit another company;
- the hero is a centered headline over an interchangeable blob;
- every section is a heading followed by three equal cards;
- the imagery could belong to any industry;
- the motion has no relationship to the product;
- the copy relies on adjectives instead of evidence.

The design passes when its silhouette, hierarchy, and one signature interaction remain recognizable in grayscale without the logo.
