# Anti-patterns

## Generic AI visual output

Reject:

- centered headline, gradient blob, two buttons, three cards;
- a card around every paragraph;
- excessive pills and badges;
- decorative glassmorphism;
- random purple-to-blue gradients;
- interchangeable dashboard mockups;
- floating abstract spheres with no brand relationship;
- fake statistics or logo clouds;
- section labels and numbers that add no navigation value;
- identical section spacing and alignment from top to bottom;
- motion applied uniformly to every element.

## Traditional marketing output

Reject:

- a full-width stock-photo banner with text over it;
- long navigation with every service exposed;
- alternating left-image/right-copy sections repeated mechanically;
- dense paragraphs centered across the viewport;
- ornamental icons used as proof;
- a carousel hiding primary content;
- a footer-sized CTA after every section;
- desktop-first layouts that merely stack on mobile.

Alternating media bands can be useful, but they need variation in scale, crop, content job, or spatial behaviour.

## False modernity

Dark mode, 3D, parallax, oversized type, gradients, and animation are materials, not a direction. Use them only when they reinforce brand, meaning, or interaction.

## Over-designed controls

Do not sacrifice:

- recognizable links and buttons;
- readable form labels;
- focus visibility;
- target size;
- state clarity;
- predictable navigation;
- content access without animation.

Distinctive marketing composition should sit around familiar controls, not make controls mysterious.

## Reference cloning

Never copy a well-known brand's complete combination of colour, typography, radius, layout, motion, and voice. Extract principles and transform them through the current product's identity and constraints.
